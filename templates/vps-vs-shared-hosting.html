HOSTING · UPGRADE DECISION

VPS vs shared hosting: check what an upgrade actually changes

Keep shared hosting if it supports the job you need. Consider a VPS when you can name a specific limit and verify that the selected plan removes it. Do not buy on the word “VPS” alone: DreamHost's Managed VPS does not provide root access, and DigitalOcean offers both shared-CPU and dedicated-CPU Droplets. Root-access evidence; CPU evidence. Our interpretation: treat operating-system permissions, CPU allocation and migration dependencies as separate purchasing questions. A positive answer to one does not answer the others. This is the decision rule used below, derived from the named examples rather than a claim that every shared or VPS plan behaves alike.

Write an upgrade ticket before comparing plans

Instead of starting with a provider ranking, complete this sentence: “My current plan cannot do ___; the candidate plan explicitly permits ___; I will verify it by ___.” Keep a missing answer as Unconfirmed. A larger resource card is not the missing answer.

This worksheet is our proposed workflow. It records your own requirements and observations; its empty fields are intentional. It does not represent a test of your site or of a hosting service.

Example: you need a package that requires administrator access

This is an illustrative requirement, not a tested customer case. Put the package's actual installation requirement in the ticket. If it really requires root, a plan documented as having no root does not satisfy that requirement, even when its name contains VPS. If the software can instead run inside your user directory, investigate that route before assuming that administrator access is necessary.

DreamHost's root-access article explicitly discusses installing software in a user's home directory without root. That is a documented possibility, not proof that your particular package will work. Official source. Reviewed 2026-09-28.

Example: you want more predictable processing capacity

Write down the workload and the symptom first. Then compare the allocation policy of the actual candidate plans. Do not mark “exclusive CPU” confirmed merely because the page says virtual server. For a candidate with a shared-CPU policy, decide whether that policy meets your requirement instead of silently treating it as dedicated capacity.

The DigitalOcean distinction in the evidence table provides a concrete place to check that question. It does not establish the performance of a particular site. No latency, throughput or capacity measurement was performed for this article.

Example: the website moves, but the bundle does not

Make a dependency row for each service your site relies on: its website files, database, email and recovery copy. For each row, write “retained,” “moved,” or “replaced,” and identify who will check it. An unanswered email row is a migration question, not a reason to assume email continues unchanged.

Use separate acceptance checks: open the migrated site, test its essential interactions and verify the services you decided to retain. These are suggested checks, not claims that we completed your migration. DreamHost's instructions say to confirm that the site works on the destination before cancelling the old plan. Official migration instructions. Reviewed 2026-09-28.

Turn the ticket into a decision

Stay for now when you have not identified a requirement that the current arrangement fails. Investigate a VPS when a candidate's documented capability addresses that requirement and you have an owner for the work. Pause the purchase when the deciding capability is still Unconfirmed. These are editorial decision options, not promises about cost or performance.

Keep the completed ticket with the evidence date. If the provider changes a plan, recheck the deciding capability rather than rewriting the whole decision around a new headline. Compare the plan against the original task, not against the excitement of a new specification.

Questions before switching

Does VPS always mean root access?

No. DreamHost's Managed VPS is a documented counterexample. Check the particular product instead of generalizing from the label. Official source. Reviewed 2026-09-28.

Does VPS always mean dedicated CPU?

No. DigitalOcean documents shared-CPU and dedicated-CPU Droplets. Check the allocation model of the selected plan. Official source. Reviewed 2026-09-28.

What traffic number means I must upgrade?

This page does not set one. Our worksheet asks you to identify a specific limitation and an acceptance check; it does not turn visitor count into a universal hosting requirement.
